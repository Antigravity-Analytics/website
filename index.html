The company

Real work is still done by people with their hands.

Computers keep getting smarter. We build software that helps them do it better: cooking, building, fixing.

We watch how the work really happens. Then we turn it into clear steps and clear choices. And a person always makes the final call.

But physical work is still unmeasured where it happens. Software knows what was sold and what was ordered. It does not know what was used, what was wasted, or what hands actually did. That gap wastes money and scarce labour every day. So we build physical AI setups inside real operations, kitchens first, to capture that truth and create production data that teaches machines how humans work.

Kitchens

Food prepared, food sold, and food wasted are three different numbers in most kitchens, and only one of them is in the system.

Construction

What was contracted, what is claimed complete, what is accepted complete, and what is approved to pay are four different states, and most projects track them as one.

Mechanics

A vehicle knows a great deal about its own fault. Most of that record never reaches the job log, the estimate, or the parts list in a usable form.

How we build it

Each step funds the next, and feeds it.

We do not start with a robot and look for work for it. We start with the work, earn the right to be inside it, learn it from the inside, and only then give that learning a body.

01

Sell the service

Back office plus service marketplace for physical businesses.

What we sell or build

For kitchens, Mise. For construction and mechanics, the same pattern in their language, a back office that runs the job and a marketplace where work and specialists are posted and hired by availability and calendar.

What it produces

Revenue, real jobs running on our system, and a trusted place inside the operation.

What it makes possible next

A real operation that will let us measure the work from inside, with consent, as part of a service it already values.

02

Capture inside the work

A universal capture kit, the same in every trade.

What we sell or build

Sensors and cameras built into things workers already wear and rooms they already work in, sold or placed through partners to better the service. Direction only today: this kit is not built yet.

What it produces

Primary data, a linked record of a real job, what hands did, what was used, in what order, and what the outcome was.

What it makes possible next

A dataset no lab study and no outside dataset contains, commercial physical work captured from inside.

03

Compare with the world

Primary data checked against secondary data, in one common form.

What we sell or build

A single representation for physical work, so kitchen, construction, and mechanic episodes can be compared and trained together without rebuilding the format per trade. Our research name for the current draft of that format is the Physical Work Episode, described in plain language on the research log.

What it produces

A clear map of where real work differs from lab and public data, and where outside data can be trusted.

What it makes possible next

Training that starts from truth about real work, not only from clean examples.

04

Train one brain

One brain that learns across jobs and across trades.

What we sell or build

A small brain first, tested on narrow, measured tasks, then widened only where the results pass. Research in progress today.

What it produces

Skills that transfer, grasping, tool use, sequencing, and recovery from errors, learned from many jobs, not one demonstration.

What it makes possible next

Intelligence that a body can use, tested back in real jobs, not only in a lab.

05

Give it a body

Partner for a body first, ship our own when the economics hold.

What we sell or build

A partnership with a mechatronics company that already builds a capable body, or, when the money and the results are there, our own robots, placed into the same trades we already serve.

What it produces

One general purpose robot that can work across the jobs it was trained inside.

What it makes possible next

Every new trade we enter becomes a new skill for robots already placed, which makes each placed robot more valuable over time.

The Antigravity staircase: service first, capture inside the work, comparison with the world, one brain, then a body. Each step is a business on its own and the training ground for the next.

Step 2, direction

One kit in every trade, so the data speaks one language.

If the kit changes per industry, the data format changes per industry, and one brain can never learn across trades. So the kit is designed to be universal. The clothes change, a kitchen mask, construction safety glasses, a mechanic work shirt. The sensors and the data points stay the same.

Sensor shirt

Torso movement, posture, and effort across a shift

Glasses or mask camera

What the worker sees, head movement, and voice. In a kitchen this sits in a mask workers already wear, so no new habit is needed

Sensor gloves

Hand and finger movement, grip, touch, and how long a hand holds a tool or material. Hands are where skilled work lives

Overhead camera

Full body movement in the space

Room camera

The whole scene, other people, objects moving, and the state of the room around the work

The business record

Order, invoice, job log, estimate, parts, and outcome, linked to the same episode, so the movement is never separated from its result

The capture kit is a design direction, not a built product. No kit is for sale, and no capture is running in a customer site today.

The asset

Lab data teaches clean examples. Real jobs teach the work.

Public and lab datasets are useful, and we use them. They are also, by design, clean, short, and separated from money and outcomes. A real commercial job is none of those things. The gap between the two is not a problem to hide. Measured properly, that gap is the asset.

The horizon

Earth is the training ground.

A machine sent far from Earth cannot count on perfect conditions or a fixed script. It will need what people know: how to cook, build, and repair. And what to do when things go wrong.

The end goal is autonomy for physical work: machines that can cook, make, and build reliably anywhere, even beyond Earth.

“When robots land on Mars, someone has to teach them to cook. We’re writing the cookbook.”

This notice explains what happens to information when you visit this website or contact Antigravity Analytics Private Limited.

1. Who is responsible

Antigravity Analytics Private Limited is responsible for the information it receives through this website and through messages sent to its published email addresses.

2. Information involved

  • Technical delivery data: IP address, browser and device details, request time, requested file, referring page and basic security logs may be processed by the site host to deliver and protect the website.
  • Correspondence: if you email us, we receive the address you use, the contents of your message and any attachments you choose to send.
  • Website preferences: this build does not create a first-party account, profile or persistent preference record.

3. Why information is used

Technical data is used to deliver the site, maintain security, diagnose faults and understand aggregate demand. Correspondence is used to answer the purpose of your message, evaluate a partnership or investment conversation, and keep a necessary business record.

4. Analytics programme

Antigravity uses Simple Analytics to understand aggregate page views, broad traffic sources and anonymous interaction events (section views, button and link clicks). The purpose is to improve the clarity, accessibility and reliability of the site, not to build advertising profiles. Simple Analytics sets no cookies, collects no personal data, and honours Do Not Track.

Advertising measurement is also present, and it runs only with your consent. If you accept in the cookie banner, the Meta Pixel loads on this site. It records page views, form submissions and booking link clicks, sets cookies, and shares device, browser and interaction data with Meta so Antigravity can measure campaign performance and show ads to people who visited this site. If you decline, the pixel does not load and nothing is sent to Meta by this site. No TikTok pixel is installed on this site. Your choice is stored in your browser local storage, and you can change it at any time from the Cookies section of this page. Advertising measurement will not capture message contents, keystrokes, clipboard contents, precise location, identity documents, sensitive attributes or screen recordings.

5. Sharing

Information may be handled by infrastructure and email providers acting for Antigravity, or disclosed where required by law. It is not sold as a personal-data list. We do not use this website to collect payment-card details, government identifiers or sensitive profile information.

6. Retention

Hosting security records are retained according to the host’s operational schedule. Email correspondence is kept only as long as reasonably needed for the conversation, legal obligations and legitimate company records. A specific deletion request may be limited where retention is legally required.

7. Your choices

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to certain uses of personal information. You may also withdraw consent where consent is the basis of processing.

8. Contact

For a privacy question or request, write to hello@antigrav.si. Do not send identity documents unless we specifically confirm they are necessary and provide a secure route.
