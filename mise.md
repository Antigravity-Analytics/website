## The whole back office, in one place.

The morning brief is the part you open every day. Under it sits the whole back office: counts, invoices, orders, credits, waste, and the month-end books. Here is all of it.

The morning

- Morning briefThe day starts with one short brief: what might run out tonight, what yesterday cost you, and what needs a decision.

- Rush-risk warningsMise works out which items will not last through tonight’s rush, hour by hour, and puts them at the top of the brief.

- Store health scoreOne plain score in the brief shows how the store is doing today.

- Yesterday’s moneyEvery leak from yesterday is listed with its proof one tap away: the invoice, the count, and who entered it.

Counts and stock

- Guided countsA two-minute count of only the items Mise flags, zone by zone (walk-in, dry store, line), with cases, packs and loose units added up for you. You never do the math.

- Opening countBefore the first brief, an opening count sets the starting numbers, and you sign off the recipes, pack sizes and counts yourself.

- Count reviewWhere a count and the expected number disagree, you accept, split or correct the reason (used, wasted, overcharged, missing) before anything adjusts.

- Recipes and costingRecipes turn your sales into ingredient use. Each store can edit yields, sub-recipes and modifier rules like “extra bacon,” with costing kept up to date.

- Batch prepPrep batches are logged on the floor, and when sales pass a full batch, Mise records the next batch as made, so stock stays honest between counts.

- Expiring batchesBatches carry their shelf life, and the day’s expiring batches show on the floor pad before they become waste.

- Menu drift queueA new or changed item on your till that is not linked to ingredients raises a flag with a suggested link. Nothing slips past the stock math unlinked.

- Sunday spot-checkA short weekly check of the five highest-value items, so small losses surface before month end.

On the floor

- Floor PIN sessionsStaff open their own session with a Floor PIN and land on a floor pad with big tap targets, not the owner’s brief.

- Receiving checksAt the back door, staff check what arrived against what was ordered, while the truck is still there.

- Waste loggingWaste is logged in seconds on the pad, with who logged it, so yesterday’s money can show where food went.

- Panic-buy captureWhen something runs out mid-rush, the emergency buy is captured on the pad: a photo of the receipt, the amount, and which item ran out.

- Panic spend lineEmergency buys are priced against the wholesale price and reported as their own line, so you see exactly what running out costs.

- Food safety recordsTemperature and holding logs, batch shelf life and allergen records, kept as an evidence pack that is ready for inspection.

Invoices and suppliers

- Invoice checkingForward invoices by email or upload them. Mise reads them, flags duplicates and price changes, and checks every line three ways: against the order and against the delivery.

- Photo-side verificationAny line Mise is unsure of, including handwriting on a paper bill, opens side by side with the photo, so you confirm or fix it yourself. Nothing posts on a guess.

- Dispute draftsWhen an invoice is wrong, Mise drafts the dispute with the evidence attached, ready for you to send.

- Credits ledgerEvery promised supplier credit is tracked until the credit actually lands on a later invoice. A credit that never arrives stays open, aging where you can see it.

- Supplier catalogueYour supplier list builds itself from your invoices: pack sizes, prices, and who supplies what.

- Shrinking packsIf a supplier quietly shrinks a pack, the cost per unit gives it away, and the change is flagged.

Orders

- One proposed orderOne order, worked out from your stock, expected demand, delivery days, pack sizes and prices, with its reason in one plain sentence.

- Approval of exact contentsNothing is ever sent without your yes on the exact contents. Change anything and it needs a fresh yes. Retries never send an order twice.

- Minimums and cutoffsSupplier minimums and order cutoffs count down on the approval screen, with a reminder before a cutoff is missed.

- Unacknowledged ordersIf a supplier does not confirm an order in time, the order shows it, with the next step named (call, or send it elsewhere). Never silent waiting.

The books

- Savings ledgerA running ledger of what Mise has caught and corrected for the store, month by month, in plain dollars.

- Month-end packAt month end, every movement traces back to its evidence, ready for your accountant. Mise is not your accounting system; it hands over a clean pack.

- Delivery-platform checksMonthly statements from the delivery apps are checked line by line (sales, minus the platform’s cut, minus refund clawbacks), and fault-waste claims are tracked until they land.

- Availability adviceIf an item runs out, Mise advises pausing it on the delivery apps. It never pauses anything on its own.

Repairs and creative help

- Facilities marketplaceWhen equipment breaks, hire vetted local trades from listings that show their papers in the open. You pay the provider directly; Mise keeps the booking and the record, and never touches the money.

- Creative marketplaceVideographers, photographers and menu designers, hired the same way: papers in the open, paid directly by you, and every campaign’s spend approved by you first.

Anywhere

- Works offlineCounts and logs keep working with no connection, saved safely on the device, and send when the connection returns. Nothing is lost, and nothing is doubled.

- Honest connection statesIf a connection breaks, the screen says so, says when it last worked, and marks anything waiting as queued, never as sent.

- Web, phone and tabletThe same Mise on the web, iPhone, iPad and Android, in light or dark, with controls that stay easy to tap.

- Roles and assigned storesOwners, managers and floor staff each sign in with their own role and see their own stores.

## Asked in the order they block the call.

What does setup ask of me?

Four steps, at your pace. You enter your own data with our tooling, walked through together. The minimum to start fits in one shift: your Clover authorisation (read only, granted by you on the call), one recent supplier invoice, your recipes as they stand, and a time for a first count. Does it work with my POS?

Clover connects today. Other systems are in pilot or not yet, and the fit check above gives the plain answer for yours. If yours is not live, the waitlist writes to you the day it opens. No date is promised. Is it finished?

No, and we would rather say so. Mise is in its founding pilot, set up store by store. The first value is not the finished product. It is the invoice check on the call, run on one of your own invoices. What does it cost?

Pricing is per location, and it is explained whole on the 15-minute call, in your own invoice numbers: what it costs, what it has to save, and the terms, in the same order for every store at the same stage. In Canada the terms are in Canadian dollars; in the United States, in US dollars. Nothing is charged on this page, and no card is taken here. How does the trial work?

The pilot opens with a 14-day trial. A card is saved when the trial starts and is charged only when the trial ends, and you get a warning three days before the first charge. The data rights terms land in writing before the trial starts, so nothing about the card or your records is a surprise. What if it does not save more than it costs?

On day 30 you get a written proof report on your own records, worked against the baseline set in the first checked week of your pilot: what was checked, what was found, what it saved. There is no guarantee and there are no refunds. We would rather you judge the report than a promise. What happens to my data?

The details you send in the form are used to answer your request and run your pilot, and nothing else. Your store’s records stay yours. The data rights terms are given in writing before any trial starts, and before a card is saved, so you can read them before anything begins. Support, when you ask for it, sees what the owner sees and changes nothing: access is read only, agreed with you, and time-boxed, and emergency access is declared to the owner at the moment it happens. Every company that touches your invoices is named in those terms, including the service that reads them.

Leaving

## Leaving is as plain as joining.

Cancelling is exactly as easy as signing up. There is no retention maze and no call you have to sit through to leave. Fees are non-refundable, and that is stated in the contract before you sign. Your records on exit stay yours and stay exportable for twelve months after you leave, with a warning before anything is deleted. Come back inside the year and everything is restored exactly as you left it. Coming back is on the intro terms of the day you return, never a worse rung for having left. Founding terms are not promised on a return; they belong to the cohort that stayed. Marketing emails end with one click or one reply. The words are the same in every footer: unsubscribe at any time.
